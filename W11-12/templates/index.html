Why Scaling is Critical for PCA and t-SNE
PCA maximizes variance along orthogonal axes. If features have drastically different ranges (e.g. Proline up to 1680 vs. Nonflavanoid phenols at 0.5), the components will be dominated entirely by the highest-scale feature regardless of information content. StandardScaler normalizes each variable to mean 0 and unit variance, ensuring equal geometric contribution.

Methodological Foundations

Principal Component Analysis (PCA): A linear, unsupervised transformation that projects high-dimensional data onto orthogonal axes that maximize variance. PCA is deterministic, global, and inductive (allows transforming new points).

t-SNE: A non-linear, probabilistic technique that minimizes the Kullback–Leibler divergence between pairwise similarities in high-dimensional space and Student-t distributed similarities in low-dimensional space. Preserves local neighborhoods but does not preserve global distances.

Common Interpretation Pitfall: Do not judge cluster size or distance between clusters in t-SNE as physical geometric truth; density changes and perplexity settings alter apparent visual separation.
