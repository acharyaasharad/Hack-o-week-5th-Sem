Bagging vs Boosting Architecture Comparison

Parallel independent averaging vs sequential error-focused boosting

Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregation)

Trains multiple base learners (e.g. Decision Trees) independently in parallel on bootstrap resamples of the dataset. Predictions are combined by majority vote (classification) or averaging (regression).

Primary effect: Drastically reduces variance of unstable high-variance base learners without increasing bias.

Boosting (Gradient Boosting / XGBoost / LightGBM)

Trains shallow weak learners sequentially. Each subsequent model fits the pseudo-residuals (gradients) of the previous ensemble stage. Shrinkage (learning rate) prevents individual trees from over-correcting.

Primary effect: Reduces both bias and variance; requires careful regularization (e.g. tree depth, subsample, reg_alpha, reg_lambda).

Core Machine Learning Concepts

1. What is the Bias–Variance Tradeoff? ▼
Bias refers to the error introduced by approximating real-world complex problems with an overly simplified model (e.g. fitting a straight line to quadratic data). High bias causes underfitting.

Variance refers to a model's sensitivity to small fluctuations in the training set (e.g. a deep decision tree that memorizes noise). High variance causes overfitting, characterized by a large gap between training performance and validation performance.
2. Why does Random Forest beat a Single Decision Tree? ▼
An individual decision tree is low bias but high variance (unstable). Random Forest decorrelates trees by selecting a random subset of features at each split and training each tree on a bootstrap sample. When averaging predictions across many decorrelated trees, the variance decreases by a factor proportional to 1/M, where M is the number of trees.
3. How do XGBoost and LightGBM Regularize Boosting? ▼
Traditional gradient boosting relies mainly on learning rate and shallow trees. XGBoost and LightGBM add explicit loss penalty terms: reg_alpha (L1 penalty on leaf weights, enforcing sparsity) and reg_lambda (L2 penalty, smoothing leaf weights). LightGBM additionally uses histogram binning and leaf-wise tree growth with depth limits to maintain speed and control complexity.
4. Data Leakage Safeguards in Practice ▼
Target leakage occurs when a predictor feature incorporates information that is unavailable at prediction time or directly derived from the target.
• UCI Bank Marketing: The duration attribute describes the call length, known only after the call concludes. Including it artificially inflates accuracy.
• UCI Bike Sharing: casual and registered ride counts sum directly to the target cnt. Including them is circular.
