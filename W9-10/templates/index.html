Why is Preprocessing Crucial for Distance-Based Clustering?
Clustering algorithms like K-Means and DBSCAN compute Euclidean distance in $n$-dimensional space ($d = \sqrt{\sum (x_i - y_i)^2}$). If features are not scaled, a category with high variance (e.g. Fresh spending up to $112,000) will dwarf features with smaller amounts (e.g. Delicassen up to $47,000). StandardScaler normalizes each dimension to mean $\mu=0$ and variance $\sigma^2=1$, giving equal geometric weight to each purchasing behavior.

          ('encoder', OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown='ignore'))
      ]), categorical_features)
  ])),
  ('clustering', KMeans(n_clusters=4, random_state=42))

    Methodological Guide: Unsupervised Clustering

    K-Means: Partitions data into $k$ spherical clusters by minimizing inertia (sum of squared Euclidean distances to centroids). Fast and inductive ($O(n \cdot k \cdot i)$).

    Hierarchical (Agglomerative): Bottom-up tree grouping using linkage criteria (e.g. Ward minimizes variance). Generates dendrograms but is non-inductive and quadratic in memory ($O(n^2)$).

    DBSCAN: Density-based spatial clustering that separates core points from noise (outliers with label -1). Does not require pre-specifying $k$ and finds arbitrary cluster shapes.

    Silhouette Score: Measures how similar an observation is to its own cluster compared to neighboring clusters ($-1$ to $1$).
